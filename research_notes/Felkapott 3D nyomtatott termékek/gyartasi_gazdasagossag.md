# Production economics of popular sellable FDM 3D-printed products (Bambu Lab P1S/P2S/A1 class), 2026

Research note: these hosts could not be reached in this session (DNS blocked): makerworld.com (direct fetch), wiki.bambulab.com, 3djake.hu, trendlytic.io, makers101.com. Data on grams and print time per item is thin, and print times depend heavily on slicer profile and scale.

## 1. Typical print time, grams, material, AMS need and post-processing per product type

### Takeaway
Hard per-model data exists for only some categories. Crystal dragons run 2–7.5 h and are close to 200 g for the full-size version. A Gridfinity 1x1 bin takes about 20–25 min and 10–13 g. A personalized keychain takes about 20 min. Lithophanes take 2–5 h. Small, fast, single-material items (keychains, Gridfinity, small flexis) have the lowest material cost per item. Multicolor AMS prints add purge waste and time.

### Cited Findings
**Flexi / articulated animals, including the crystal dragon**
- "Consistent Size Crystal Dragon (Commercial Use)" on MakerWorld (model 995026): the whole print is "just under 200 grams". It uses flushing objects ("flushies") to cut purge waste to about 13 g. — [MakerWorld 995026](https://makerworld.com/models/995026)
- "Improved Crystal Dragon" (MakerWorld 655976): the designer says it uses only about 5 g more than the original despite being larger. Printing two dragons with flushies on one plate adds just under 1 hour. — [MakerWorld 655976](https://makerworld.com/en/models/655976-improved-crystal-dragon)
- A smaller articulated crystal dragon (remix of Molodos v2) is listed at 2 h on one plate (0.2 mm layers, 15% infill). — [MakerWorld 2524087](https://makerworld.com/models/2524087)
- Magnetic crystal dragon remix: 5.7 h on one plate (0.2 mm standard profile). — [MakerWorld 2438115](https://makerworld.com/models/2438115)
- Articulated Crystal Spine Dragon: 7.5 h in its print profile. — [MakerWorld 2142966](https://makerworld.com/models/2142966)
- Original Cinderwing3D crystal dragon on Thangs: slices to 13 h 25 min. One user reported it closer to 11 h in practice. — [Thangs Cinderwing3D Crystal Dragon](https://thangs.com/designer/Cinderwing3D/3d-model/Crystal%20Dragon-1060509)
- A costed flexi dragon example assumes about 100 g PLA and 5 h of printing, sold at $18. The author says the weight and time are guesses. — [3D Printing Substack](https://3dprinting.substack.com/p/what-does-one-3d-printer-actually)
- Blog estimate for flexi animals: 1–3 h print time. The niche is called "saturated, price collapsed". — [Trendlytic via search snippet](https://www.trendlytic.io/blog/3d-printed-items-to-sell-on-etsy)

**Keychains / NFC tags**
- A personalized keychain takes roughly 20 min to print. Blogs call keychains "lightweight, quick to produce". — [3dsearch.net](https://3dsearch.net/blog/best-selling-3d-printed-items-etsy-2026?lang=en), [hyper3d.ai](https://hyper3d.ai/blog/best-selling-3d-printed-items)
- One blog claims about 396 monthly sales at $7.20 for a keychain listing, with no methodology given. — [3dsearch.net / hyper3d search snippet](https://hyper3d.ai/blog/best-selling-3d-printed-items)

**Gridfinity bins**
- Gridfinity Bin 1x1x5U "Fast print": 23 min and 10 g PLA (0.2 mm layers, 2 walls, 15% infill). — [MakerWorld 182968](https://makerworld.com/en/models/182968-gridfinity-bin-1x1x5u-fast-print-labeled-light-sta)
- A 1x1 "all heights" multi-model file (heights 1–9U) takes 2.8 h and 81 g across two plates. — [MakerWorld 428020](https://makerworld.com/en/models/428020-gridfinity-bin-1x1-all-heights)
- One guide says a 1×1 bin is "around 8–15 g". Another gives averages of "about 22 minutes … and about 13 grams of filament per grid square". — [Qidi Gridfinity guide](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/gridfinity-organizers-print-now); [GridPilot filament cost](https://gridpilot.us/blog/gridfinity-filament-cost)
- A multi-size Printables Gridfinity bin set takes 3 h 53 min and 132 g PLA. — [Printables 1305693](https://www.printables.com/model/1305693-2025-gridfinity-bin-12-latest-fix-with-more-sizes)
- Custom Gridfinity sets on Etsy are priced at $12–$40 per set. — [hyper3d.ai / get3dstl snippet](https://get3dstl.com/profitable-3d-prints-etsy/)

**Lithophanes / lamps**
- Lithophane print time is estimated at 2–5 h and runs unattended. — [Trendlytic via search snippet](https://www.trendlytic.io/blog/3d-printed-items-to-sell-on-etsy)
- Lithophane photo keychains on Etsy sell for about $9.54–$20. Custom portraits sell for more. — [Etsy lithophane keychain market](https://www.etsy.com/market/lithophane_keychain)

### Inferences
- Lithophanes need white PLA and a 100% (or near) infill effect from wall thickness. They are single-material, so no AMS is needed. The lamp version adds hardware (LED, base), and that cost is not counted here.
- Multicolor AMS prints (crystal dragon in gradient/silk colours, multicolour signs) add purge waste per colour change and extra time. The crystal dragon example shows purge waste can be cut to about 13 g with flushing objects. Printing several items per plate spreads each colour swap across more items.
- Planters and vases in vase mode, cookie cutters, phone stands, cable organizers, board game inserts, pet tags, car accessories, wall art, mini figurines and brackets: no sourced figures were found (see Gaps). As rough guidance only, they behave like these neighbouring categories: cookie cutters and pet tags like keychains (small, thin, under 1 h), vase-mode planters like a single thin wall (fast for their size, low infill grams), board game inserts like Gridfinity (high grams, long time, but low labour).

### Gaps
- No sourced grams or print times were found for vase-mode planters, cookie cutters, phone/headphone stands, cable organizers, board game inserts, pet tags, car accessories, wall art, mini figurines, spare parts or Christmas ornaments. The search tool ran out of budget, and makerworld.com and printables.com could not be fetched directly. Slicing sample models in Bambu Studio is the reliable way to fill these.
- No sourced post-processing times per product were found.
- Fidget toys: no specific data was found.

## 2. Hungarian filament prices (HUF/kg) and electricity cost per printer-hour

### Takeaway
Budget PETG in Hungary costs about 3,900–7,300 Ft/kg (Alza, Árgép). Bambu PLA Basic costs about €23 per roll on the official EU store, or €17–20 per roll when buying 4 or more. Hungarian retailer HUF prices for Bambu filament could not be retrieved. The household electricity tariff is about 36.4 Ft/kWh within the subsidized limit and about 70.1 Ft/kWh above it. A P1S draws roughly 100–120 W on PLA, which works out to only about 4–8.5 Ft per printer-hour.

### Cited Findings
- Bambu Lab official EU store, PLA Basic: base price €23.38 (another snippet shows €22.99). With 4+ rolls the price starts at €16.99/€19.99 per roll. There is also a promo code for an extra €10 off filament orders over €120. The page does not say when these offers end. — [Bambu Lab EU store PLA Basic](https://eu.store.bambulab.com/en-lt/products/pla-basic-filament)
- Third-party EU retail price for Bambu PLA Basic 1 kg: €28.99–30.99 at aio.lv (Latvia) and €29.95 at LDLC (France). — [aio.lv](https://aio.lv/en/product--bambulab-bml-24630--11972987.md); [LDLC](https://www.ldlc.com/en/product/PB00727302.html)
- PETG 1 kg in Hungary: Landu PETG 3,890 Ft incl. VAT (cheapest on Alza); eSUN PETG-Basic 5,050 Ft on Alza and from 5,060 Ft on Árgép (plus 1,200 Ft shipping); Creality PETG 6,810 Ft; generic PETG about 7,020–7,290 Ft; recycled rPETG 6,490 Ft. This looks like a roughly spring 2026 snapshot. — [Alza PETG](https://www.alza.hu/EN/cheap-petg-filaments/18872450-p5.htm); [Árgép eSUN PETG](https://www.argep.hu/ar-osszehasonlitas/500003145984_esun-petgbasic-175-mm-1000.html)
- Filament prices are falling. A January 2026 survey says competition is pushing PLA "into commodity range", and that Bambu's 10-spool discount only lands it in the average price range. — [Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/news/fff-filament-prices-continue-to-fall-as-competition-pushes-pla-into-commodity-range)
- Hungarian residential electricity (A1 tariff): 36.386 Ft/kWh gross up to 2,523 kWh per year (about 210 kWh per month), and 70.104 Ft/kWh above the limit. — [Pénzcentrum 2025](https://www.penzcentrum.hu/otthon/20250513/lakossagi-aram-ara-2025-mennyibe-kerul-1-kw-aram-1178679)
- The residential network-use fee is frozen for 2026. — [Economx](https://www.economx.hu/magyar-gazdasag/2025/11/13/befagyasztas-rendszerhasznalati-dij-kormany-aram-819261/)
- P1S power draw on PLA: about 105 W average (Filamino, citing the Bambu wiki); about 120 W measured (Igor's Lab); 11 W idle. — [Filamino](https://filamino.com/blog/3d-printer-electricity-cost); [Igor's Lab](https://igorslab.de/en/bambu-lab-p1s-ams-combo-3d-printer-in-test-setting-the-bar-a-bit-higher-again/6)
- Another source gives 150–250 W averages for P1-class printers, which is higher than the measured figures. — [colourlabelprinter](https://colourlabelprinter.pages.dev/how-much-electricity-does-a-3d-printer-use/)

### Inferences
- Electricity per printer-hour: 0.10–0.12 kWh × 36.4–70.1 Ft ≈ 4–8.5 Ft/h. Even at 0.25 kWh/h and the above-limit price it is about 18 Ft/h. Electricity is negligible next to filament. Note that a print farm will quickly exceed the 2,523 kWh subsidized limit, and a business (company) contract is priced differently. Business tariffs were not researched.
- HUF conversion (assumes about 385–400 Ft/EUR, not sourced): Bambu PLA Basic is about 6,500–7,700 Ft/kg with the bulk discount and about 9,000 Ft/kg at the base price. That is about 6.5–9 Ft per gram. Budget PETG is about 3.9–7.3 Ft/g.
- Example material costs at 7–9 Ft/g PLA: Gridfinity 1x1 (10–13 g) ≈ 70–120 Ft; crystal dragon (about 200 g) ≈ 1,400–1,800 Ft; flexi dragon (about 100 g) ≈ 700–900 Ft. Add purge waste for AMS prints.

- No 2026-dated electricity tariff confirmation was found. The 36.4/70.1 Ft figures come from a 2025 source, and the freeze suggests they still apply.

## 3. Margin per printer-hour benchmarks from print-farm sellers

### Takeaway
Published benchmarks sit around $1.5–2.5 of gross profit per printer-hour, with a wide spread from $0.50 to $5. Labour (plate removal, packing) is the real bottleneck, not machine time.

### Cited Findings
- One farm operator targets at least $2 per print-hour per machine. Actual gross profit varied from about $0.50 to $5 per print-hour. — [3dprinterly](https://3dprinterly.com/are-3d-print-farms-profitable-how-to-make-them-more-profitable/)
- A worked example of a 13-hour print gives $1.54 per print-hour, with machine depreciation at about $0.15/h. — [pea3d ROI calculator](https://pea3d.com/en/3d-printing-profitability-bambu-lab-roi-analysis/)
- Maintenance reserve: $0.08–0.15 per print-hour (one guide); about $0.10/h (another). — [pea3d cost calculator](https://pea3d.com/en/3d-printing-cost-calculator-bambu-lab-efficiency-guide/); [3dprinterly](https://3dprinterly.com/are-3d-print-farms-profitable-how-to-make-them-more-profitable/)
- Flexi dragon example: $18 sale price, about 100 g PLA, 5 h ≈ $2.55 per print-hour before ad costs and operator labour. Single-colour flexi dragons on Etsy list for about $11–23 depending on size. — [3D Printing Substack](https://3dprinting.substack.com/p/what-does-one-3d-printer-actually)
- The production bottleneck is usually part removal and plate swap time, not print speed. — [BambuHub production guide](https://bambuhub.net/guides/production-printing)
- Flexi animal prices reportedly fell about two thirds since 2023. — [hyper3d.ai / get3dstl snippet](https://hyper3d.ai/blog/best-selling-3d-printed-items)
- Case study: the CruiseCup brand made $808,000 with a Bambu A1 farm. That is a volume example, not a per-hour margin. — [makers101 CruiseCup](https://makers101.com/cruisecup-3d-print-farm/)

### Inferences
- Short, light, personalized items (name keychains, pet tags, NFC tags, signs) likely give the highest margin per printer-hour. Their material cost is under about 100 Ft, they print many per plate, and personalization supports higher prices. They do need more labour per item (order handling, packing).
- Long, heavy prints (crystal dragon at 5–13 h and about 200 g) tie up the printer. At a typical 6,000–10,000 Ft retail price (assumed HU equivalent of $11–23) they give lower per-hour returns, and the market is saturated.
- Gridfinity and organizers: very cheap per unit and low labour (no colour change, stackable plates), but low per-unit prices. They work as sets.

### Gaps
- No Reddit print-farm threads were found in search. No Hungarian seller benchmarks were found.

## 4. Licensing (MakerWorld / Printables commercial use) and trademark risk

### Takeaway
Most MakerWorld files carry the Standard Digital File License, which covers personal use only. Selling prints requires the creator's Commercial License Membership (a monthly fee) or a model explicitly marked for commercial use. Selling trademarked or copyrighted characters (Pokémon, Disney) carries real takedown and shop-shutdown risk.

### Cited Findings
- The default MakerWorld Standard Digital File License allows printing for personal use only. Selling requires a creator's Commercial License Membership, which has a monthly fee. — [All3DP legal guide](https://all3dp.com/1/things-to-3d-print-and-sell-a-legal-guide/)
- The commercial membership covers physical prints only, never the digital files. It covers all of that creator's models on MakerWorld. Commercial sales must stop once the membership ends. — [MakerWorld community post](https://makerworld.com/en/community/post/1740451); [MakerWorld Commercial License Membership Agreement](https://makerworld.com/en/creator-membership-agreement)
- Bambu Lab launched the "Commercial License Membership" in 2025. — [All3DP](https://all3dp.com/4/bambu-lab-launches-makerworld-commercial-license-membership/); [3Printr](https://www.3printr.com/flexible-subscription-models-makerworld-adapts-commercial-license-for-3d-printing-services-5885581/)
- Some models are explicitly labelled for commercial use, e.g. "Consistent Size Crystal Dragon (Commercial Use)". — [MakerWorld 995026](https://makerworld.com/models/995026)
- Most popular flexi dragons are licensed designs, which limits who can sell them. — [3D Printing Substack](https://3dprinting.substack.com/p/what-does-one-3d-printer-actually)
- Forum users find the Standard license wording ambiguous. This is community opinion, not an official ruling. — [Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/understanding-makerworlds-standard-digital-file-license/257609)
- An uploader's chosen license does not legalize trademarked characters. A Mickey Mouse figurine is likely to infringe even with a commercial license. — [All3DP legal guide](https://all3dp.com/1/things-to-3d-print-and-sell-a-legal-guide/)
- Pokémon rights holders enforce through cease-and-desist letters, DMCA takedowns, marketplace complaints, and sometimes litigation. — [Vondran Legal](https://www.vondranlegal.com/pokemon-ip-defense)
- Etsy acts on takedowns filed by rights holders. Sellers can lose their whole shop. Fan art is not automatically fair use. — [eRank](https://help.erank.com/blog/can-you-sell-fan-art-on-etsy/); [InsightAgent](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/selling-copyrighted-characters-brands-on-etsy)

### Inferences
- The "Print & Sell" label could not be confirmed in the sources. The confirmed mechanisms are the Commercial License Membership and per-model commercial labels.
- For Printables, the license is whatever the model page states (often Creative Commons variants). CC-NC (non-commercial) licenses forbid selling. This was not verified in this session.

### Gaps
- Membership tier prices could not be retrieved (makerworld.com was blocked).
- No Hungarian or EU-specific enforcement cases were found.

## 5. Batching tips (plates per print, multiple items per plate)

### Takeaway
Fill the plate with as many items as fit, especially in multicolour prints so purge waste per item falls. Plate swap and part removal are the throughput bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- Printing two crystal dragons plus flushies on one plate adds just under 1 h compared with one, and flushies cut purge waste to about 13 g. — [MakerWorld 655976](https://makerworld.com/en/models/655976-improved-crystal-dragon); [MakerWorld 995026](https://makerworld.com/models/995026)
- Plate removal and swap time, not print speed, is usually the bottleneck. — [BambuHub](https://bambuhub.net/guides/production-printing)
- Flexi animals sell best in multipacks. — [hyper3d.ai](https://hyper3d.ai/blog/best-selling-3d-printed-items)

### Inferences
- Use extra build plates so the next job starts while the last one cools. Schedule long prints overnight and short, labour-heavy batches during working hours. Group by colour to cut AMS swaps.
